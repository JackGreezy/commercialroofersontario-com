Roofing Subcontractor for General Contractors in Ontario, CA

Roofing Subcontractor Support for GCs Building in the Inland Empire

A general contractor building a distribution center or a tenant improvement in Ontario needs a roofing sub who returns a bid on schedule and shows up on the day the schedule says, not a week after. We work new construction, shell-and-core, and tenant improvement roof scopes for GCs across the Inland Empire on that basis.

Bid Turnaround That Fits a Construction Schedule

Most bid packages we see have a roofing line item due before the estimator has finished the structural steel takeoff. We staff estimating to turn around plan-and-spec bids fast, and we flag ambiguities in the roof spec before bid day rather than after award, when a discrepancy becomes a change order fight instead of a clarification. On design-build projects we will work directly from a performance spec and propose a system that hits the reflectivity, wind uplift, and warranty targets the project needs.

We price the same way for a 50,000-square-foot tenant improvement and a 500,000-square-foot shell building: unit cost by system, clearly separated mobilization and equipment line items, and alternates called out so your value-engineering pass has real numbers to work from instead of guesses.

Holding a Position in the Critical Path

Roofing sits on the critical path of almost every ground-up project — nothing dries in, no interior trades start, no certificate of occupancy happens until the roof is watertight. We build our crew and material lead times around the GC's actual schedule, not a generic production rate, and we flag material lead-time risk on specialty systems (certain PVC or coated metal profiles run longer lead times than standard TPO) as early as pre-construction so it does not surface as a schedule surprise in month four.

When weather or upstream trade delays push our start date, we replan crew size to protect the milestone rather than just sliding the whole roofing duration and hoping it absorbs elsewhere in the schedule.

Spec Compliance and Warranty Administration

A roof spec on an Inland Empire project usually calls out a specific manufacturer system, a wind uplift rating, and a Title 24 reflectivity requirement tied to climate zone 10. We install to the letter of that spec and handle the manufacturer paperwork — inspection scheduling, warranty registration, closeout documentation — so the GC is not chasing a subcontractor for submittals during closeout. Systems we install regularly carry coverage from Carlisle, GAF, Holcim Elevate, Johns Manville, Sika Sarnafil, and Versico, and we know each manufacturer's inspection and documentation requirements well enough to avoid a coverage gap discovered a year after turnover.

Permitting jurisdiction is worth confirming early on any Inland Empire project, since the market straddles San Bernardino and Riverside counties and adjacent cities do not always interpret cool-roof and reflectivity requirements identically. We track those local variations so the roof spec we submit matches what the plan checker in that specific jurisdiction expects, rather than assuming a permit that cleared in Ontario will clear the same way in a neighboring Riverside County city.

What Changes Between Ground-Up and Tenant Improvement Work

The considerations shift depending on project type:

  • Ground-up shell buildings need roof-to-wall and parapet coordination locked down before the envelope trades mobilize
  • Tenant improvements over an existing occupied building need tie-ins to a roof that may already be under warranty, which requires manufacturer notification before cutting into it
  • Cool roof compliance documentation differs for a full re-roof versus a partial TI scope under current Title 24 rules
  • Rooftop equipment coordination on a TI project usually means sequencing around a live HVAC or electrical trade already on site
  • Punch-list roof items on occupied buildings need scheduling around tenant hours, unlike a vacant shell

One Point of Contact From Bid to Closeout

GCs juggling several Inland Empire projects at once do not want a different roofing foreman's phone number for every job. We keep the same project lead from bid through final inspection, so questions about a change order or a warranty document go to someone who was on the project from day one, not a dispatcher reading a file for the first time.

Questions GCs Ask Us

How fast can you turn around a bid on a plan-and-spec package?

Most complete bid sets get a response within the standard bid window your project requires. Tell us the bid date up front and we will flag immediately if the roof spec has gaps that need an RFI before we can price it accurately.

Can you work as a design-assist sub on a design-build project?

Yes. We will propose a system and detailing package that meets the performance criteria in the RFP, with cost-per-square-foot options at different warranty terms so your team can present alternatives to the owner.

What happens if our schedule slips and pushes our roofing start date?

We track that risk from award and communicate lead-time exposure early, particularly on specialty systems. If your schedule moves, tell us as soon as you know so we can replan crew and material delivery rather than absorbing the slip silently.

Do you handle warranty registration and closeout paperwork yourselves?

Yes, that is part of the scope on every project. We manage manufacturer inspections, register the warranty, and hand your team a closeout package rather than leaving submittals for someone else to chase.

Can you tie a new TI roof into an existing warranted roof without voiding coverage?

Usually, if we notify the manufacturer before cutting into the existing membrane and use compatible materials at the tie-in. Skipping that notification is the most common way a GC accidentally voids an owner's existing roof warranty on a TI job.
